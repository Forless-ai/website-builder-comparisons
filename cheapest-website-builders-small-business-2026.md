**Last reviewed: August 18, 2026**

Finding the cheapest website builder for a small business is not simply a matter of finding the lowest advertised price.

Free plans may not support a custom domain. Promotional prices may increase substantially at renewal. And some inexpensive builders are designed primarily for one-page websites rather than a broader small-business presence.

This comparison therefore focuses on a practical question:

**What does a small business need to pay to publish a professional website using its own domain?**

We compare FORLESS, Carrd, Hostinger, Webnode, Weebly, Squarespace, and Wix based on published pricing, custom-domain support, hosting, SSL, AI capabilities, and intended use case.

- SSL
- Forms

- Analytics

- Multiple sites

Landing pages, personal profiles, portfolios, and businesses whose entire website can comfortably fit into a focused one-page format.

Carrd's $9/year Pro Lite plan is cheaper than its Pro Standard plan, but it is not directly comparable here because custom-domain publishing requires Pro Standard or higher.

## 4. Webnode — From $4.50/Month

Webnode provides both free and paid website-building options.

Its **Limited plan currently starts at $4.50 per month** and allows users to connect their own domain.

However, the Limited plan does not include every Webnode feature. For example, Webnode's current pricing information indicates that its AI Assistant is not included with Limited.

## 5. Weebly — Paid Plan Required for Custom-Domain Publishing

Weebly offers free website creation, but businesses wanting to publish using their own custom domain need a paid plan.

Its Personal plan has been published at **$13 per month when billed annually**, with higher-tier Professional and Performance plans available for businesses requiring additional functionality.

Weebly combines general website-building capabilities with ecommerce functionality.

### Best for

Businesses looking for a familiar website-building environment with broader commerce functionality than a simple landing-page builder.

## 6. Squarespace — From $19/Month

Squarespace operates in a different segment from ultra-low-cost website builders.

Current Squarespace website subscriptions start at approximately **$19 per month**, with higher plans providing additional functionality.

Hosting is included, and eligible annual website subscriptions can include a custom domain for the first year.

Squarespace emphasizes professional presentation, design flexibility, website management, and commerce capabilities.

### Best for

Businesses and brands that prioritize polished design, presentation, and a broader professional website platform over obtaining the lowest possible annual cost.

## 7. Wix — From About $17/Month

Wix provides one of the broadest website-building ecosystems in this comparison.

Its current pricing page displays the Light plan at approximately **$17 per month** on an annual subscription, although Wix explicitly notes that prices and currencies vary by location.

Wix provides hosting, templates, drag-and-drop editing, AI-assisted website creation, marketing functionality, and increasingly advanced business capabilities on higher plans.

Those capabilities can include ecommerce, payments, scheduling, and other business tools depending on the selected plan.

### Best for

Businesses that expect to need a larger website ecosystem, deeper customization, ecommerce, bookings, payments, or more sophisticated business functionality.

## Free vs Cheap: An Important Distinction

A free website builder is technically cheaper than every paid product in this comparison.

But free and professional website setups are not necessarily equivalent.

Free plans commonly involve limitations such as:

- Platform-branded domains
- Platform branding
- Reduced storage or bandwidth
- Limited business functionality
- Restrictions on advanced features

For example, Webnode's free websites use a Webnode subdomain, while Carrd's custom-domain functionality requires Pro Standard or higher.

Therefore, this comparison focuses primarily on the cost of publishing a professional website using a custom domain rather than simply identifying which platforms offer a $0 plan.
